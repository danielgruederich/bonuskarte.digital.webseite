**In short:** Push notifications are the most direct line you have to a customer's lock screen — and used with relevance and restraint, they're the cheapest way to turn a loyalty card into repeat visits.

You've handed out loyalty cards. Customers are collecting stamps. But a card sitting quietly in a Wallet doesn't bring anyone back on its own. What closes the loop is the ability to reach out at the right moment — and for a local business, nothing does that as cheaply or directly as a push notification. The catch is doing it in a way people welcome rather than mute. Here's how.

## Why push beats every other channel for local shops

Think about the alternatives. Social posts reach a fraction of your followers and depend on an algorithm. Email gets buried. Paid ads cost money every time. A push notification, by contrast, lands directly on the lock screen of someone who already chose to keep your card — no ad spend, no algorithm, no inbox to fight through.

And customers prefer this channel. In the **DACH Loyalty Report 2026** (hello again × Handelsverband), **71.5% prefer a digital loyalty card over a printed one**, and the digital format is the one that lets you communicate at all. Paper can't send a message. Digital can — for free.

## Customers want to hear from you (really)

The fear every business owner has is being annoying. The data is reassuring: **82.1% of customers said they want updates at least weekly.** People who like your shop don't resent hearing from it — they resent being spammed with things that don't apply to them.

The other two numbers to keep in mind:

- **61.0% want personalised offers.** Generic blasts underperform; a message that fits the customer wins.
- **56.5% value proximity relevance** — being reached when they're actually nearby.

Put together, these say something simple: send fewer, more relevant messages, and your customers will be glad you did.

## What to send: five notifications worth reading

Not every message earns its place on someone's lock screen. These five almost always do.

**1. The "reward unlocked" nudge.** When a customer earns a free coffee or a discount, tell them. It's the single most effective push you can send, because it gives them a concrete reason to come in today — and it feels like good news, not marketing.

**2. The "you're almost there" reminder.** "You're two stamps from a free lunch." This works because it taps the same instinct that makes people finish a loyalty card: an open loop they want to close.

**3. The quiet-day offer.** If Tuesdays are slow, send a small, time-boxed reason to visit on Tuesday. You're smoothing your own demand curve using the customers you already have.

**4. The new-product announcement.** A new seasonal drink, a fresh menu, a limited batch. Regulars *want* to know — this is the content that makes a notification feel like a favour.

**5. The proximity reminder.** When a customer is physically near your shop, a location-based card can surface on their lock screen. It's the digital equivalent of a friendly wave as they pass the window.

## How often — and how to not overdo it

A weekly cadence is a healthy baseline for most local businesses. It matches what customers ask for and keeps you top of mind without training people to swipe you away. A few rules keep you on the right side of the line:

- **Every message should pass the "would a regular be glad to get this?" test.** If not, don't send it.
- **Prefer relevance over reach.** A personalised nudge to 40 people beats a generic blast to 400.
- **Watch your opt-outs and redemptions.** They're your honest feedback. Rising opt-outs mean slow down; rising redemptions mean you've found the rhythm.
- **Time it for action.** A lunch offer at 11 a.m. beats the same offer at 3 p.m.

## No app required

The best part for a local shop: none of this needs a custom app. Loyalty cards in **Apple Wallet and Google Wallet send push notifications directly to the lock screen** without any download. The customer added your card in one tap, and the ability to reach them came with it — the reach of an app with none of the install friction.

## The bottom line

Push notifications are the payoff of going digital. A loyalty card gets people to join; notifications are what actually bring them back. Customers have told us they want to hear from the businesses they like — at least weekly, personalised, and relevant to where they are. Respect that, keep every message worth reading, and you turn a passive card into a steady stream of repeat visits. You can test the whole thing free for 90 days and watch which notifications your customers actually respond to.
