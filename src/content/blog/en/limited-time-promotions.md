**In short:** Limited-time promotions work because a real deadline converts a vague "sometime" into a decision today, and the fastest way to deliver them is a push notification straight to the customer's wallet card, which is exactly what most customers say they want, weekly.

There is a difference between a customer who likes you and a customer who comes back this week. The gap between them is a reason to act now. Most of the time, your regulars have a loose intention to return "sometime," and sometime keeps sliding. A limited-time promotion is the tool that collapses "sometime" into "today," and it works because of two of the most reliable principles in human behavior.

## Why a deadline changes behavior

**Scarcity** makes things feel more valuable. When an offer is available only for a short window, it stands out from the endless background of things you could do eventually. The limited window itself signals worth.

**Loss aversion** makes the pull stronger. People feel the sting of missing out roughly twice as intensely as they enjoy an equivalent gain. "Free topping this weekend only" is not really framed as a gain to be won; it is framed as something you will lose if you do nothing. Doing nothing suddenly has a cost, and that cost is what gets a customer off the sofa.

Put together, a genuine deadline does something a standing discount never can: it makes now feel different from later. That is the entire job of a promotion, and it is why "this weekend only" outperforms "ten percent off, always."

## Keep the deadline real

The power comes entirely from the limit being true. The moment customers learn that your "this week only" offer quietly runs every week, the scarcity evaporates and you have simply trained them to wait for the deal. A permanent promotion is not a promotion. It is a lower price with a countdown graphic.

So protect the mechanism:

- Make the window short and specific: a weekend, a single day, a defined date.
- Honor the deadline. When it is over, it is over.
- Give each promotion a real reason to exist so it does not feel arbitrary.

## What to actually promote

The best limited-time offers are tied to something genuine, which makes them feel like an event rather than a discount:

- **Seasons and weather:** a hot-day offer at the ice cream shop, a warming special on a cold week.
- **Slow days you want to fill:** a Tuesday-only reward to smooth out a quiet midweek.
- **Local events:** something tied to a match, a market, a festival, a holiday.
- **Milestones:** a birthday reward for the customer, or an anniversary of your shop.
- **Restock or launch moments:** a new item available at a special rate for its first weekend only.

Each of these gives the customer a reason that is easy to understand and easy to act on, which is what turns interest into a visit.

## Send them more often than you think

Here is the number that surprises most owners. According to the 2026 DACH Loyalty Report, **82.1 percent of consumers want to be informed at least weekly.** Not monthly. Weekly.
> Source: hello again × Handelsverband, DACH Loyalty Report 2026.

The fear of "bothering people" is largely misplaced, as long as the content is relevant and the deadline is genuine. Customers who joined your program are telling you they want to hear from you regularly. Silence is the bigger risk: a program that messages its members twice a year is a program most members have forgotten. A steady, weekly rhythm of small, real, time-limited offers is not spam. It is what your customers signed up for.

## Deliver it where it lands instantly

A time-limited offer is only as good as its delivery. An email sent to a deadline-driven promotion often sits unread in a crowded inbox until the window has closed. The fix is to reach customers where the message is immediate.

A **push notification to a digital loyalty card** in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet arrives directly on the lock screen. No app to download, no inbox to fight through, no algorithm deciding whether it is shown. For a "this weekend only" offer, that immediacy is the whole point, the message reaches the customer while there is still time to act on it. It also reaches exactly the right people: the ones who already opted in by adding your card.

### Why do limited-time promotions work so well?

Because a deadline turns a someday intention into a today decision. Two well-documented principles are at play: scarcity, where things feel more valuable when availability is limited, and loss aversion, where missing out feels worse than the equivalent gain feels good. More often than most owners expect. The 2026 DACH Loyalty Report found that 82.1 percent of consumers want to be informed at least weekly. Provided the content is relevant and the deadline is genuine, a weekly rhythm matches what customers actually want rather than annoying them.
