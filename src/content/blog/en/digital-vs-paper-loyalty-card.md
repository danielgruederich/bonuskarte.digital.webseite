**In short:** Paper loyalty cards are cheap but passive — they sit in a drawer and do nothing, while digital cards live in the customer's Wallet, send reminders, and hand you real data about who comes back and when.

The paper punch card is a classic for a reason. It costs a few cents to print, everyone understands it instantly, and there is no technology to learn. Behind almost every café counter, there is a little stack of them.

But once you look past the low print cost, the picture changes. Most businesses only notice the gap after a few months — when they realise how much of the loyalty they paid to build is quietly leaking away. Here is what actually changes when you move from paper to digital.

## What is wrong with the paper loyalty card?

The problem is not the idea. The problem is that paper is passive. It can't remind, it can't measure, and it can't protect itself.

**It gets lost and forgotten.** A large share of paper cards are never fully redeemed — they end up in a washing machine, an old jacket, or a kitchen drawer. For the customer that's frustrating. For you, the reward you were funding never turns into the repeat visit it was supposed to buy.

**It gives you zero data.** How many active collectors do you have right now? Which weekday drives the most redemptions? Which promotion actually moved people? With paper, you're guessing. Without data, there is nothing to improve.

**It is easy to fake.** Any customer with a hole punch or a matching stamp can top up their own card. In a competitive high street, that adds up.

**It can't talk to your customer.** A paper card sits silently in a drawer. It can't tell a regular you've got a new seasonal drink, or nudge them on a slow Tuesday.

## What does a digital loyalty card do differently?

A digital card sits in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet — apps that are already installed on every phone. That single fact changes everything downstream.

**It can't be lost.** The card is on the phone, and the phone is always there. On the commute, at the market, on the walk home. There is no "I left my card at home."

**It's measurable.** A dashboard shows active users, stamp frequency, redemption rate, and growth in real time. You stop guessing which offer worked and start seeing it.

**It's fraud-proof.** Every stamp is encrypted and bound to the customer's device. It can't be added manually or copied.

**It can reach out.** You can send a push notification straight to the lock screen — a reminder, a reward that just unlocked, a quiet-day offer. Location-based cards can even surface on the lock screen when a regular walks past your door.

## Do customers actually want this?

They do, and the shift is not subtle. In the **DACH Loyalty Report 2026** (hello again × Handelsverband), **73.8% of customers said they would switch to a digital solution**. Preference for the format itself is just as clear: **71.5% prefer a digital loyalty card over a printed one**, versus 28.5% who still favour print.

This matters because loyalty is not a nice-to-have. In the same report, **29.3% of people say they shop more often at a business that has a loyalty program**, and **27.9% say they've spent more since they started using one**. The card isn't just a thank-you — it changes behaviour.

## When does paper still make sense?

Honesty matters, so here are the cases where paper still earns its place:

- **A predominantly older clientele** that isn't used to smartphones.
- **One-off events and pop-ups**, where you're not trying to build long-term loyalty.
- **As a backup** next to the digital card, for the rare guest who doesn't use a Wallet.

These are edge cases. Your typical regular already has their phone in their hand at the counter — and increasingly expects to handle everything from it.

## Should you run both in parallel?

Many businesses that start with a digital card keep the paper version around for the first few weeks. That's a sensible bridge, not a failure. In practice, clear patterns show up fast: the digital cards get carried more, used more, and redeemed more — because they're always there and they remind people to come back.
