**In short:** Wallet loyalty cards use the Apple Wallet and Google Wallet apps that are already on every phone — so customers add your card in one tap, with no download, no account, and no password.

The single biggest reason loyalty programs fail isn't a bad reward. It's friction. Ask someone to download an app, create an account, and remember a password just to collect a stamp, and most people quietly decline. Wallet cards remove every step of that. Here's how they actually work — and why they've become the format customers prefer.

## What is a Wallet loyalty card?

Every iPhone ships with **Apple Wallet**, and every Android phone with **Google Wallet**. They're the apps most people already use for boarding passes, event tickets, and payment cards. A Wallet loyalty card is simply your stamp card, points card, or membership card living in that same place — a native card on the phone, not a webpage and not a separate app.

Because the Wallet app is already installed and trusted, there is nothing for the customer to set up. That's the whole trick.

## How a customer gets your card (in one tap)

The flow is deliberately boring, which is the point:

1. **The customer scans a QR code** — on a table tent, a sticker at the till, a receipt, or a link you send them.
2. **A page opens with an "Add to Apple Wallet" or "Add to Google Wallet" button.** The phone detects which one to show.
3. **They tap once.** The card is now in their Wallet.

No download. No email verification. No password. From "never heard of you" to "card in pocket" takes about ten seconds — and that low friction is exactly why Wallet cards get adopted where branded apps stall.

## How stamps, points, and rewards get added

Once the card is in the Wallet, updating it is just as simple. At the counter you scan the customer's card or they scan your code, and you confirm the action — a stamp, a set of points, a redeemed reward. Within seconds the card updates on their phone.

Two things make this better than paper:

- **It's fraud-proof.** Every stamp is encrypted and bound to the customer's device. It can't be added manually after the fact, and the card can't be copied or shared.
- **It updates itself.** When a reward unlocks, the card's front can change to say so, and a notification can appear on the lock screen. The card is alive, not a static piece of card stock.

## The quiet superpower: notifications without an app

Here's what surprises most business owners. A Wallet card can send push notifications directly to the lock screen — the same reach a native app has — without anyone installing an app.

That means you can:

- Nudge a regular on a slow afternoon.
- Announce a new seasonal product.
- Tell someone a reward just unlocked, so they come in to claim it.

Customers welcome this when it's relevant. In the **DACH Loyalty Report 2026** (hello again × Handelsverband), **82.1% want updates at least weekly** and **61.0% want personalised offers.** Some Wallet cards can also surface on the lock screen when a customer is physically near your shop — and **56.5% of people value that kind of proximity relevance.** You get app-level communication with zero install friction.

## Why customers now prefer it

This isn't a niche preference. In the same report, **71.5% of customers said they prefer a digital loyalty card over a printed one**, against just 28.5% who still favour print. The reasons line up with everything above: it's always in their pocket, it can't be lost, and there was nothing to install.

## What you need to set it up

Less than you'd think:

- **One card design.** You create a single loyalty card; the platform generates both the Apple Wallet and Google Wallet versions automatically. You never manage two systems.
- **A way to scan.** A phone or tablet at the counter is enough — no special hardware.
- **A QR code to hand out.** Put it on a sticker, a table tent, or a receipt, and you're live.

There's no printing, no app store submission, and no developer. Setup takes minutes, not weeks.

## The bottom line

Wallet loyalty cards win because they respect the customer's time. They ride on the Wallet apps people already have, so there's no download, no account, and no password — just one tap to join and a card that can't be lost. You get the reach of an app, the security of encryption, and the format that most customers now say they prefer. If you've been putting off a loyalty program because "nobody wants another app," this is the answer: there is no app. You can try it free for 90 days and see how many people add the card in the first week.
