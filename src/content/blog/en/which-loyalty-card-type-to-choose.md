**In short:** The right loyalty card depends on three things — how often your customer buys, whether your price is fixed or variable, and whether you're rewarding visits, spend, or membership. Match those and the choice is obvious.

"Digital loyalty card" usually makes people picture a stamp card: buy ten, get one free. That's the classic, but it's far from the only option. There are five distinct card types that suit local businesses, and each solves a different problem. Pick the wrong one and your program feels off; pick the right one and it runs itself. Here's each type, who it fits, and how to decide.

Loyalty is worth getting right, not just running for its own sake. In the **DACH Loyalty Report 2026** (hello again × Handelsverband), **29.3% of customers say they shop more often where a loyalty program exists** and **27.9% say they've spent more since using one.** And according to **Antavo's 2025 research, loyalty programs return around 6.2x ROI in the DACH region.** The upside is real — so let's match the mechanism to your shop.

## 1. The stamp card

**What it is:** The customer collects one digital stamp per visit or purchase. After a set number, they get a reward — usually a free product.

**Best for:** Cafés (10 coffees = 1 free), bakeries, juice and bubble-tea bars, quick lunch spots — anywhere with a low unit price and high visit frequency.

**Why it works:** It's dead simple to understand, and the "almost there" feeling is a powerful motivator. If your customer visits several times a week and each purchase costs about the same, this is your card.

## 2. The points card

**What it is:** Instead of a fixed stamp, the customer earns points proportional to spend — for example, one point per euro. Rewards unlock at point thresholds.

**Best for:** Restaurants, delis, bookshops, wine merchants, florists — businesses where the basket size varies a lot from visit to visit.

**Why it works:** It rewards bigger spenders faster, which nudges larger baskets. When one guest spends €12 and another €45, points are fairer and more motivating than a one-size stamp. The trade-off is that it's slightly less instantly intuitive than a stamp.

## 3. The cashback card

**What it is:** The customer earns back a percentage of what they spend as store credit they can redeem on a future visit — say 5% back, banked on the card.

**Best for:** Higher-value or occasional-purchase businesses — boutiques, specialty retail, salons with premium services — where a free product doesn't fit but a running credit does.

**Why it works:** Store credit is a strong reason to come back specifically to you rather than a competitor, because the reward is only spendable in your shop. It also scales naturally with spend, like a points card, but feels more like real money to the customer.

## 4. The membership card

**What it is:** A digital membership pass that identifies the customer as a member and grants access, status, or perks. No stamps or points — just identity and belonging.

**Best for:** Gyms and studios (a digital pass replacing a plastic card), co-working spaces, clubs and associations, wine or book clubs.

**Why it works:** It replaces plastic membership cards entirely — no app, no login, no reprints — and it doubles as a communication channel. New classes, schedule changes, and member perks arrive as a push notification on the lock screen. It's about ongoing relationship, not a countdown to a freebie.

## 5. The gift card

**What it is:** A digital card loaded with a fixed value that one person buys and another redeems — sent by link and added to the Wallet in a tap.

**Best for:** Florists, restaurants, beauty studios, and retail of every kind — anywhere people buy presents.

**Why it works:** Gift cards bring in brand-new customers who'd never have walked in otherwise, and the giver has effectively pre-paid you. There's no plastic, no envelope, and nothing to lose — the recipient taps a link and the card is in their Wallet.

## Quick comparison

| Card type | Ideal business | Customer gets | You get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stamp** | Café, bakery, lunch spot | Free product after X visits | Higher visit frequency |
| **Points** | Restaurant, retail | Points per euro, flexible rewards | Larger baskets |
| **Cashback** | Boutique, premium services | Store credit back on spend | Strong reason to return |
| **Membership** | Gym, club, co-working | Digital pass + member perks | No plastic, a comms channel |
| **Gift** | Florist, restaurant, retail | Easy to gift by link | New customers, prepaid revenue |

## How to decide in three questions

You don't need to overthink this. Answer three questions:

1. **How often does your customer buy?** Daily or weekly → stamp card. Occasionally → points, cashback, or membership.
2. **Is your price fixed or variable?** Fixed → stamp card. Variable → points or cashback, so bigger spenders are rewarded faster.
3. **Are you selling membership or access?** Yes → membership card. Selling presents too? Add a gift card alongside it.

Many businesses combine two types — a gym with a membership card for access and a stamp card for its café, or a restaurant with a points card plus gift cards for the holidays. The rule of thumb is simple: add a second card type only when you have a second, genuinely different buying pattern to reward.

## The bottom line

There's no universally "best" loyalty card — only the best fit for how your customers actually buy. Match the mechanism to your frequency, your pricing, and what you're rewarding, and the program starts working on its own. Every type here lives in Apple Wallet and Google Wallet with no app to download, and you can test whichever fits free for 90 days before committing to anything.
