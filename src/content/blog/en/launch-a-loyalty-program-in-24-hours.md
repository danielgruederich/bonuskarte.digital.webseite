**In short:** The technical setup of a digital stamp card takes under 15 minutes. The reason to give it a full day is everything around it, the counter script, the signage, and your first message, which is what actually turns the launch into returning customers.

You do not need a project plan, a developer, or a budget to run a loyalty program. You need one focused day. Here is exactly how to spend it, broken into four blocks. Follow it in order and you will be live tonight and stamping real customers tomorrow.

## Block 1 (about 1 hour): Decide the offer

Do not open any tools yet. The most common launch mistake is designing the card before deciding what it is for. Answer three questions first.

**What is the reward?** Pick one thing that is genuinely wanted and cheap enough to give away repeatedly. For a cafe, a free coffee. For a bakery, a free loaf or pastry. For an ice cream shop, a free scoop. Avoid vague rewards like "a surprise." People chase a clear finish line, not a mystery.

**How many stamps to earn it?** Eight to ten is the reliable range. Fewer feels too easy to matter; more feels too far to start. When in doubt, choose ten and consider giving the first stamp free at sign-up so nobody starts at zero.

**Who is it for?** Your regulars first. A loyalty program is not about winning strangers. It is about giving the people who already like you a reason to come a little more often. Write the offer as one sentence you could say out loud: "Collect ten stamps, get a free coffee." If you cannot say it in one breath, simplify it.

## Block 2 (about 15 minutes): Build the card

Now the fast part. On bonuskarte.digital, create your account. No credit card is needed to start. Then build the card:

- **Upload your logo** as a PNG or JPG.
- **Set your brand color** so the card matches your shop.
- **Add your business name** and an optional subtitle, like your neighborhood or city.
- **Set the stamp rule:** the number of stamps and the reward you decided in Block 1.
- **Enter your address** so the card can surface on the customer's lock screen when they are nearby.

Save. The system instantly generates a shareable link and a QR code. That link is your entire program. There is nothing to install and nothing for customers to download. This is genuinely where most of the "hard" work ends.

## Block 3 (about 2 hours): Prepare the launch, not just the tech

This is the block owners skip, and it is the one that decides whether the program works. A live card that nobody knows about collects no customers.

**Write your counter script.** The highest-converting moment is at payment. Decide the exact sentence your team will say and make sure everyone uses it: *"Have you got our digital loyalty card yet? Ten stamps and your next coffee is on us, it takes ten seconds to add."* Practice it once so it sounds natural, not like a pitch.

**Print and place the QR code.** Put it where people already look while waiting or paying: on the counter, at the till, on the table. One clear sign beats five hidden ones. Add a short line of text under the code so it explains itself: "Scan to collect stamps. No app needed."

**Prepare your channels.** Draft the posts you will publish once you go live:

- An Instagram story with the QR code and one line of copy.
- The link added to your Instagram bio and your Google Business Profile.
- A message ready for your WhatsApp broadcast list if you have one.

**Brief your team.** Walk them through scanning a customer's card, which takes about three seconds, and what happens when a card is full. If your staff are confident, customers will be too.

## Block 4 (rest of the day): Go live and get the first ten cards

Publish everything. Put the sign on the counter. Post the story. Then set one small, concrete goal for the day: **ten cards added.** Not a hundred. Ten real regulars is a successful launch, because they are the people who will actually come back and fill the card.

For every customer at the counter today, use the script. When someone adds a card, thank them and, if the system allows it, give them their first stamp on the spot so they leave already underway rather than at zero. That single move, starting people at one instead of zero, meaningfully increases the number who finish the card.

By the end of the day you will have a live program, signage in place, your team trained, and your first cards in real wallets. Tomorrow you simply keep asking.

## The 24-hour checklist

- [ ] Reward decided (one clear, popular item)
- [ ] Stamp count set (eight to ten)
- [ ] Account created and card designed
- [ ] QR code printed and placed at the counter
- [ ] Counter script written and practiced
- [ ] Instagram, Google Business and WhatsApp prepared
- [ ] Team briefed on scanning and redemption
- [ ] First ten cards added

## Häufig gestellte Fragen

### Can I really launch a loyalty program in one day?

Yes. The setup itself takes under 15 minutes: create an account, design the card, set the stamp rule, and copy the link. The rest of the 24 hours is spent on the things that make the launch actually work, like writing your counter script, printing the QR code, and preparing your first message to customers.

### Do my customers need to download an app?

No. The card lands straight in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet. Customers scan a QR code once, tap add to wallet, and they are done. No app, no password, no account to create. This is the single biggest reason digital cards get adopted faster than app-based programs.

### What do I need before I start?

Very little. Your logo as a PNG or JPG, your brand color, a decision on the reward, and a smartphone or tablet at the counter to scan customer cards. You do not need a developer, new hardware, or changes to your point-of-sale system.

### How many stamps should the first program have?

Keep it simple for launch: eight to ten stamps for one clear, popular reward. A free coffee, a free scoop, a free lunch. You can add tiers, coupons and push campaigns later. On day one, one card and one reward is enough to start collecting real customers.

## The takeaway

Launching in 24 hours is realistic because the technology is no longer the hard part. The card is built in minutes. The day is really about the offer, the script, and the signage, the human details that decide whether the program fills wallets or sits unused.
